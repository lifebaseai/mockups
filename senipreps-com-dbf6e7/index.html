78%

Nitrogen or ammonia?

Nitrogen in the air does nothing. 78 percent of what we breathe is nitrogen.

The problem is the compounds nitrogen forms, and ammonia is one of them. It forms when manure and urine mix, it's volatile, and it settles again further away. Too much ammonia is toxic and bad for the environment. That's what "nitrogen" in farming is really about.

Why at the source

We act one step earlier

Today's solutions act after the problem has formed. We make sure it never forms.

Platform technology

One technology, two applications

We chose two key applications from a broader range of possibilities.

Poultry

Healthier animals

Chickens that eat a lot of feed get ammonia in their blood. Their liver has to clear it, and that costs energy that doesn't go into growth. Because we stop ammonia from forming, the birds stay healthier.

Ammonia reduction

Lower emissions

Where too much nitrogen settles, grass, brambles and nettles take over and heathland disappears. We tackle the forming of ammonia, instead of the far less effective approach of capturing emissions afterwards.

Where we stand

Two steps done, two running

Development is under way. Here's the state of play, step by step.

2 completed2 running1 in preparation

  1. 01Completed

    The right inhibitor found

    We vaccinated llamas, which then made antibodies. From millions of antibodies in their blood, we selected the most effective. Two patent applications were filed for the discovery.

  2. 02Completed

    Trials with faeces

    In trials with faeces, our peptides stopped the enzyme from working, and so prevented nitrogen emissions.

  3. 03Running

    First animal trials

    Our peptides were fed to pigs with no negative effects. The trials also showed they need better protection through the stomach.

  4. 04Running

    All the way through the gut

    With Wageningen Research, we're working to make the peptide travel the whole digestive tract and stay protected and active on the way.

  5. 05In preparation

    Poultry trials

    Pilot production of our peptide is running in South Africa. Trials with chickens are being prepared there, small-scale first and then at a large poultry company.

About SENI Preps

A Dutch biotech company

A biologist's idea and an economist's company.

SENI Preps was founded five years ago. Medical biologist Dr. Gerrit Keizer came up with this biological solution to the nitrogen problem by combining technologies he had worked with over a career of almost forty years.

A professor put him in touch with Ir. Marco Pieterse from Wageningen, who looks after the business and operational side. With that, the company was born.

A committee of specialists saw that this was a different solution from all the barn solutions that already existed.

Our name: Stik Er Niet In. It's a nod to the nitrogen problem (in Dutch, nitrogen is "stikstof").

Founder & CEO

Wageningen economist. Background in agrifood finance, investment, international agribusiness, and building companies on a scientific basis.

Founder & Scientific Advisor
