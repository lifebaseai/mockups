Where copper still hides

Four systems that stop working when the line goes

These life-safety systems were designed decades ago to run on copper telephone lines, often called “POTS” (Plain Old Telephone Service).

SystemWhat it does todayWhen copper is cut

Fire alarm systems

Commercial fire panels report alarms to the monitoring station over an analog line.

The panel can’t dial out. Fire emergencies won’t be reported.

Elevator emergency phones

Required life-safety phones inside elevator cabs that connect to emergency responders.

Passengers trapped inside can’t call for help.

Building security systems

Gate intercoms, access control dialers and security alarm panels still using copper lines.

Security systems lose their connection to dispatch centers.

Pool emergency phones

Required poolside emergency phones in condos, hotels and multi-family properties.

A required phone that no longer works is a code violation.

Non-functional life-safety systems trigger red tags, fines and occupancy restrictions from fire marshals and building inspectors, plus insurance issues and liability for owners and managers.

The copper line retirement timeline

South Florida is in the active retirement zone

  1. 2015

    AT&T begins POTS retirement

    Copper landline service starts retiring in select markets, beginning where fiber is available.

  2. 2018

    FCC approves copper retirement

    Carriers are no longer required to maintain analog infrastructure.

  3. 2020–2023

    Widespread disconnections

    Owners report sudden outages for fire alarms and elevator phones, with minimal notice.

  4. Now

    Critical action window

    Service is being discontinued with as little as 30 to 60 days’ notice, and sometimes none at all.

$150–300 / line / month

What some properties now pay to keep a single copper POTS line. Modern cellular and VoIP replacements are more reliable, cost less per month, and meet code requirements.

Who’s responsible?

Don’t assume someone else is handling this

Ownership of analog line replacement is often unclear, and that’s dangerous.

If you own or manage the building, you need to take action now.

You

Property owners & managers

You’re responsible for code-compliant life-safety systems. If the line fails, you’re liable, even if the telecom provider discontinued service.

Your vendors

Fire alarm & elevator companies

They maintain the equipment, but telecom line changes often fall outside their scope until you report a service failure.

The carrier

AT&T, Lumen and others

They’re retiring copper with minimal notice, and they’re not required to make sure your life-safety systems have a replacement.
